This document explains where Địa Thế's location analysis comes from, how it is calculated, and what it should **not** be taken to mean. Please read it before relying on any result to make a decision.

## 1. What an Analysis Result is

An Analysis Result is reference information about the surroundings of a point on the map, calculated from open data and third-party data. Examples: distance to amenities, roads and transit stops; estimated population density; elevation; modelled river flooding; air quality at the nearest station.

A result is **not**:

- a legal conclusion on ownership, zoning or legal status of a property;
- an official valuation, appraisal or transaction price;
- a technical assessment of construction, ground conditions, safety or environment;
- investment or financial advice, or a promise about future value;
- an assessment of people, community or local security.

Địa Thế does not say whether an area is "good" or "bad". It presents what can be measured, with sources and the completeness of the data.

## 2. How results are presented

- **Only what can be measured.** Each indicator states its source, radius and confidence level.
- **Missing data is stated as missing.** Not finding a type of amenity within the radius means the data has not recorded one; it does not mean the area has none.
- **Indicators are named accurately.** For example "relative elevation" and "modelled river flood depth", rather than a verdict of "floods easily" or "does not flood".
- **Results follow the pin.** Results are calculated from the pin coordinates, not the text address. Converting an address to coordinates can be off by tens to hundreds of metres, so please check and drag the pin to the right place.
- **A price entered by a user is that user's asking or reference price**, not a market price.

## 3. Data sources

The list of sources, licences and attribution requirements is in the Data Sources document. The main groups are: maps and amenities (OpenStreetMap), estimated population (WorldPop, GHSL), air quality (open monitoring stations), terrain and elevation, modelled river flooding, land cover, and public transport stops. Sources may be added or replaced; the Data Sources document is then updated.

## 4. Known limitations

- **Uneven completeness.** Open map data is dense along main roads and in the inner city, and sparser in alleys, outer areas and small places such as informal markets or small shops.
- **Delay.** Each dataset has its own date and may not reflect new construction or recent change. The update date is shown with results where available.
- **Resolution.** Population, elevation and land-cover data are on a grid. They are good enough to compare areas, not to describe a single alley or plot.
- **Flooding.** River flood modelling does not simulate rain-driven flooding, drains or street flooding. Do not use it to conclude anything about flooding during rain.
- **Air.** Values come from the nearest station and show the distance to it. If the station is too far, Địa Thế does not assign a value to the location.
- **Zoning and parcel boundaries.** Địa Thế has no plot-level zoning or parcel boundary data. Check with the competent authority.
- **Written commentary.** The explanatory text may be written by a language model from the calculated indicators. Địa Thế is designed so that commentary adds no figures beyond the calculated data, but you should still compare it with the indicators shown. Where they differ, the indicators prevail.

## 5. What you should do

Treat results as a starting point for questions, not a conclusion. Before you decide to buy, sell, rent or invest:

- visit in person at different times;
- check legal documents and zoning with the competent authority;
- consult suitable professionals: a lawyer, a valuer, an engineer.

## 7. Reporting data errors

If you see an obvious error, such as an amenity placed in the wrong location, tell us through the Contact Channel. For open map data you can also correct it directly at the source, which improves it for everyone.
